## Overview

Mintavibe is a mobile rewards platform built around celebrity access. Users earn MV points by
watching content, playing games and referring friends, then redeem them against fan collectables:
a signed guitar, an excursion, a live session, coffee with the artist, a voucher.

The splash screen states the whole product in three words: play games, meet celebrities, earn
rewards. Everything after that has to keep that promise legible.

## Discovery and problem definition

### The problem

A rewards currency is only real if a user can answer three questions without leaving the screen
they are on. How many points do I have. What did this action pay. What will those points buy.

Most rewards apps answer the first and dodge the other two. Points accrue somewhere in a profile
tab, activities do not say what they pay until afterwards, and redemption is a separate world with
its own prices. The currency then reads as a loyalty gimmick, and engagement follows.

Mintavibe had a second problem on top. It spans four unrelated activities, video, games, social
and shopping, and each one wants to be the front door. Left unresolved, the app becomes a menu of
things that happen to share a login.

### The goal

Make the value of a point visible at every step, and give the four earning paths one architecture
so no activity outranks another.

### My role and responsibilities

End-to-end product design: research synthesis, personas and empathy maps, card sorting, the
information architecture, wireframes and low-fidelity screens, the full high-fidelity interface,
the brand and icon system, and the prototype used in testing. The app launched after prototype
feedback had been folded back in.

<Callout kind="constraint">
  A points balance that appears in different places with different formatting stops being money and
  becomes a score. One component, one position, every screen.
</Callout>

## Understanding the user

### Research summary

Two behaviours dominate. People who arrive for a specific celebrity and treat earning as the price
of admission. And people who arrive to pass time, earn without a target, and discover the celebrity
side later. The first group needs the reward to be visible from the earning screen. The second
needs a reason to care about a balance they did not set out to build.

### Personas

Rahul, a marketing professional in a city, here for celebrity content and games. Sonal, a business
owner in a suburb, here for discounts and vouchers. Their scores disagree: Rahul rates
entertainment and social interaction highest, Sonal rates shopping and personalised offers.

That disagreement is why the four earning paths carry equal weight in the tab bar. Any ranking
would have served one persona and demoted the other.

### Journey mapping

Install, see the promise on the splash, sign up, land on home, earn something small, see the
balance move, find a celebrity worth saving for, keep earning, redeem, read the invoice. The
invoice is the end of the journey and the moment the currency is proved.

### Competitive audit

Rewards apps in this space usually bury the balance in a profile tab and present redemption as a
catalogue of gift cards. That is efficient and forgettable. The differentiator here is that the
reward is a person, not a product, so the celebrity profile had to be a first-class destination
rather than a category inside a shop.

### What testing changed

Two things. The reward moved from below the fold of a video row to the row itself, because people
were opening videos to find out what they paid. And the invoice stopped being a confirmation email:
showing points and cash side by side on screen was the single clearest answer to what a point is
worth.

  app is.

## Impact, accessibility and reflection

### Accessibility

Every row target in the earning flows clears 48 by 48 device-independent pixels, because these are
screens people tap quickly and repeatedly. Point values are always numerals beside the MV mark, not
the mark alone, and gold on near-black carries the contrast without depending on hue, so the balance
survives a colourblind reader. Tier names are words, Brigadier and Colonel, rather than colour
bands. Log out is the one red control on screen, so destructive actions are never confused with
gold ones. The invoice states each figure with its own label rather than relying on column position.

### Impact and takeaways

A multi-purpose app does not need a clever home screen, it needs a currency that is worth the same
everywhere and says so. Once every earning row carried its payout and every redemption carried its
split, the architecture stopped being a negotiation between features and became a simple tree.

The next version would put a target on the balance: pick the collectable you are saving for, and let
every earning screen show how much closer that action gets you.
