## Overview

Mintavibe is a mobile rewards platform. Users earn coins by playing games, watching videos, sharing
content and shopping, then spend them on vouchers, discounts and access to events with Bollywood
celebrities, including talk shows and coffee sessions.

## The problem

A rewards app that spans entertainment, gaming, social and commerce has no natural home screen.
Each feature wants to be the front door, and every one of them pulls a different person. Left
unresolved, the app becomes a menu of unrelated things and the currency that is supposed to connect
them reads as a gimmick.

The business problem sits directly on top of it. Coins only work as a mechanic if a user can see, at
any moment, how many they hold and what those coins are worth in something they actually want.

## The goal

Give the four activity types one shared architecture and one shared currency display, and get a new
user from install to their first earned coin without a tutorial.

## My role

End-to-end product design: user research synthesis, personas and empathy maps, card sorting, the
information architecture, wireframes, the full high-fidelity interface, the brand and icon system,
and the prototype used for testing. The work ran through five phases, and the app launched only
after prototype feedback had been folded back in.

## Understanding the user

### Personas

Two people, deliberately far apart. Rahul, a marketing professional in a city, who is here for
celebrity content and games. Sonal, a business owner in a suburb, who is here for discounts and
vouchers. Each persona carries goals, needs, pain points, psychographics and behaviour patterns, and
the two sets disagree in useful ways. Rahul rates entertainment and social interaction highest,
Sonal rates shopping and personalised offers.

That disagreement is the reason the home screen gives four earning paths equal weight instead of
promoting one. Any ranking would have served one persona and demoted the other.

### Empathy maps

The maps split each persona into what they do and what they think. The most useful lines were the
frustrations, and both became interface requirements rather than backlog items.

Rewards that are hard to redeem became the rule that a voucher always shows its cost against the
balance held. Fear of missing an exclusive event became a countdown on the screen rather than a
notification that arrives too late to act on.

## Architecture and ideation

### Card sorting

The features sorted into seven groups. The result that changed the product was points and perks
refusing to sit inside account management: people treated their balance as something they owned, not
as a setting, so it got its own place in the architecture.

### Information architecture

One flow from splash through onboarding to a decision point, sign up or log in, then a home screen
that branches into the four earning paths plus shop and profile. Every earning path sits one level
below home, which is the structural expression of the single-currency idea.

### Design system

Metropolis for everything, with a near-black ground, a warm gold for the currency and anything
earned, and a violet reserved for social features. Gold is the only colour that means value, which
is why a coin balance is legible anywhere in the app. Type is set on a 1.4 line height with slightly
open letter spacing, which is what makes a dense rewards list readable on a phone.

  score.
</Callout>

## Accessibility

Touch targets in the earning flows clear 48 by 48 device-independent pixels, since these are screens
people tap repeatedly and quickly. Coin values are always shown as numerals beside the coin mark
rather than by the mark alone, and gold against the near-black ground carries the contrast without
relying on hue, which keeps the balance legible for a colourblind user. In the wallet a credit and a
debit differ by sign and by wording, not only by colour.

A multi-purpose app does not need a clever home screen, it needs a currency that is worth the same
everywhere. Once the coin meant one thing across four very different activities, the architecture
stopped being a negotiation between features and became a simple tree.
