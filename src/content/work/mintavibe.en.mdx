## Overview

Mintavibe is a mobile rewards platform. Users earn coins by playing games, watching videos, sharing
content and shopping, then spend them on vouchers, discounts and access to events with Bollywood
celebrities, including talk shows and coffee sessions.

## The problem

A rewards app that spans entertainment, gaming, social and commerce has no natural home screen.
Each feature wants to be the front door, and every one of them pulls a different person. Left
unresolved, the app becomes a menu of unrelated things and the currency that is supposed to connect
them reads as a gimmick.

The business problem sits directly on top of it. Coins only work as a mechanic if a user can see, at
any moment, how many they hold and what those coins are worth in something they actually want.

## The goal

Give the four activity types one shared architecture and one shared currency display, and get a new
user from install to their first earned coin without a tutorial.

## My role

End-to-end product design: user research synthesis, personas and empathy maps, card sorting, the
information architecture, wireframes, the full high-fidelity interface, the brand and icon system,
and the prototype used for testing.

## Understanding the user

### Personas

Two people, deliberately far apart. Rahul, a marketing professional in a city, who is here for
celebrity content and games. Sonal, a business owner in a suburb, who is here for discounts and
vouchers. Each persona carries goals, needs, pain points, psychographics and behaviour patterns,
and the two sets disagree in useful ways.

### Empathy maps

The maps split each persona into what they do and what they think. The most useful lines were the
frustrations: rewards that are hard to redeem, and a fear of missing exclusive events. Both became
interface requirements rather than backlog items.

## Architecture and ideation

### Card sorting

The features were sorted into seven groups: onboarding, entertainment, shopping and rewards, social
and community, account management, settings and support, and points and perks. That exercise is what
turned a feature list into a navigable product.

  src="/work/mintavibe/card-sorting.webp"

### Information architecture

One flow from splash through onboarding to a decision point, sign up or log in, then a home screen
that branches into play and earn, watch and earn, refer and earn, shopping, shop and profile
settings. Every earning path is one level from home, which is the structural expression of the
single-currency idea.

## Starting the design

### Wireframes

Flat screens first, to fix the position of the coin balance before any of the brand arrived. The
balance sits in the same place on every screen, which is what lets a user check it without
navigating.

<Figure
  src="/work/mintavibe/wireframes.webp"

### Design system

Metropolis for everything, with a near-black ground, a warm gold for the currency and anything
earned, and a violet reserved for social features. Gold is the only colour that means value, which
is why a coin balance is legible anywhere in the app.

  One currency, one balance component, one position on screen. The moment a rewards app shows a
  different number in a different place, the coin stops feeling like money and starts feeling like a
  score.
</Callout>

## Accessibility

Type is set on a 1.4 line height with slightly open letter spacing, which is what makes a dense
rewards list readable on a phone at arm's length. Touch targets in the earning flows clear 48 by 48
device-independent pixels, since these are screens people tap repeatedly and quickly. Coin values
are always shown as numerals beside the coin mark rather than by the mark alone, and the gold
against the near-black ground carries the contrast without relying on hue, which keeps the balance
legible for a colourblind user.

A multi-purpose app does not need a clever home screen, it needs a currency that is worth the same
everywhere. Once the coin meant one thing across four very different activities, the architecture
stopped being a negotiation between features and became a simple tree.
