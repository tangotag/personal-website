## Context & goal

Cygnus Payments is a US payments ISO moving from reselling third-party POS to owning the product.
The goal: a POS ecosystem competitive with Toast-, Square- and Clover-tier products at a price
independent restaurants accept, differentiated by AI insights, dual pricing and offline resilience.

## Users

Owner-operators who live in reports, menus and pricing. Cashiers and servers who need speed and
fewer mistakes at the counter. Kitchen staff reading tickets from three metres away. First-time
customers at a kiosk with a queue behind them. And the Cygnus support team onboarding merchants
through the back-office "Bridge" portal.

## My role & team

Sole product designer owning strategy, information architecture, flows, UI, the design system and
handoff across all five surfaces. I partnered with product and engineering on scope and sequencing.
I did not select hardware or integrate payment processors.

<Callout kind="constraint">
  Fixed hardware, offline-first operation with later reconciliation, dual-pricing disclosure rules,
  staff turnover that demands learning the system in a single shift, and kiosk accessibility (reach,
  contrast, time-outs).
</Callout>

## Constraints

Every screen had to survive the worst shift, not the demo: gloves, glare, a dropped connection in
the middle of a ticket, and a new hire on day one.

Staff turnover set the hardest one. A new hire starts on a numeric PIN, not a password, and the
same keypad pattern reappears wherever a number is entered, so the first thing anyone learns is
also the thing they reuse all day.

## Flows & information architecture

The modules map onto five surfaces. Order, modify, pay with dual pricing, send to the kitchen and
close out on the terminal. Attract, order and pay at the kiosk. Live sales, AI reports and employee
time in the admin.

## Design system

Shared tokens across POS, kiosk, KDS, customer display and admin. A large-touch component set,
explicit states for offline and queued sync, and role-based navigation so a cashier never sees the
owner's tools.

The modifier sheet is the clearest test of the system. It has to hold a required choice, several
optional ones and a running total, on a screen someone is using with one hand while a queue waits.

## Final solution

The ticket never leaves the left rail. Menu, modifiers and payment all open beside it, so a
cashier's eyes stay in one place from the first item to the receipt.

A first-time customer with a queue behind them gets one decision per screen. Nothing is required
except the order itself: the name and phone steps exist so the counter can call you, and both can
be skipped.
