## Context & goal

360SynergyTech builds regulatory technology for banks and payment businesses. Screening is the
unglamorous core of it: every customer, every payment counterparty, checked against sanctions lists,
politically exposed person registers and adverse media, continuously.

The hard part is not finding matches. It is that most matches are wrong. A common name returns
dozens of candidates, and an analyst has to decide which, if any, is their customer. Get it wrong in
one direction and you freeze an innocent account. Get it wrong in the other and you have a
compliance failure.

## Users

Compliance analysts who clear hundreds of alerts a shift and are measured on both speed and
accuracy. Their reviewers, who need to see why a decision was made months later. And the
integration engineers who consume the same data through an API.

## My role & team

Product designer for the platform: information architecture, the screening and case workflows, the
interface, the design system and the developer handoff. I worked alongside the compliance
specialists who defined the matching rules. I did not build the matching engine or choose the data
providers.

<Callout kind="constraint">
  Regulated work leaves a trail. Every decision needs a visible reason, an auditable history and a
  named owner, and none of that may slow down the analyst making it.
</Callout>

## Flows & information architecture

Three levels, always in the same order. A list of candidate matches, an entity summary, then the
underlying evidence. The analyst never leaves the case to check something and lose their place.

## Design system

One three-part layout across every screen: navigation on the left, the working area in the middle,
evidence on the right. Status colour is used sparingly and always with a text label beside it, since
red and green alone are not a safe way to convey risk.

## Handoff

The part that saved the money. Component libraries with tokens, redlines and state matrices,
including the empty, loading, error and partial-data states that a screening tool hits constantly.
With a software engineering background I wrote the handoff the way engineers read it.

## Results & learnings

<Results>
  <Metric display="−25%" label="development time via dev-ready handoff" />
  <Metric display="~$5k" label="saved per project" />
</Results>

Analysts do not want more information, they want the decision to be easier. Put the evidence one
click behind the decision, keep the layout identical on every screen, and never let colour be the
only thing carrying meaning.
