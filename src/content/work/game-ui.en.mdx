A game interface is judged in the three seconds before a match. It has to say what the player owns,
what they can do next and what it costs, without ever getting between them and the play button. This
page collects that work. Below is the piece with the most surface area.

## Legacy Battle Royal

A PC battle royale front end for Turtle Studios: the lobby players return to between matches, the
character pipeline, the season pass, the store and the settings nobody should have to think about.

Everything hangs off one dark chrome with a single acid green accent. Green means available or
selected and nothing else, which is what lets a player scan a dense screen quickly. The persistent
top bar carries identity, currency and navigation in the same place on every screen, so the lobby
never feels like a different app from the store.

### The economy

Three surfaces sell three different things, so each one states the price in the player's terms. The
currency screen prices coin bundles in real money, the season pass prices a time window, the bazaar
prices individual items. Rarity is a labelled band, never colour on its own.

### Progression and settings

Career stats are the reason a player comes back, so they get the same weight as the store. Settings
use the same controls as the rest of the game rather than a separate options dialect.
